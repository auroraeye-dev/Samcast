01 — Three gestures

Your camera is already pointed at you. Use it.

Samcast watches for a hand in your Mac's camera and counts how many fingers are extended — the one thing hand tracking reports reliably. The thumb is left out, because its position tells you how the hand is rotated rather than whether it's open. Everything else is a consequence of that.

✊

Close your hand — hand it over

If you're in a browser, the tab closes on your Mac straight away and waits to be caught — and comes back after 20 seconds if nobody catches it. Any other window starts mirroring instead.

🖐️

Open your hand — catch it

Do this at the device you want it on. Catching is always a deliberate act on the receiving device — nobody can push a screen onto you.

✌️

Peace sign — take a screenshot

Saved straight to your Desktop. This one needs Screen Recording; links work without it.

While a page is being held, gestures are deliberately ignored.

You're walking to another device, and stray hands on the way used to cancel the handoff. Cancel from the app window instead.

05 — Guardrails

A gesture is a fast way to do something. So it has to be a hard way to do it by accident.

Most of the work in Samcast isn't the casting. It's everything that stops the casting happening when you didn't mean it.

31 tested cases

It asks before handing over a live call.

Make a fist during a Google Meet, Zoom, Teams or Webex call and Samcast stops and asks first — in a panel over your browser, not buried in the app. Doing nothing means no.

The tested cases include the ones that must not prompt. A prompt people learn to dismiss unread protects nobody.

Asked once

Trust is a decision you make, and can change.

A new device asks permission the first time it tries to hand you something. Allow it and you're never asked again. Decline and it's blocked — reversibly. Every device you've decided about is listed in the app with a button to change your mind, because a prompt that can't be undone is just a trap.

20 second return

Nothing gets lost.

If nobody catches a handoff, the page comes back on its own after 20 seconds. And Samcast refuses to grab at all when there's no device nearby to send to.

06 — Why it's built this way

Built for the two feet between your desk and theirs.

Bonjour, not the cloud

Devices find each other with Bonjour over your Wi-Fi — the same mechanism AirDrop and printers use. No internet, no accounts, no server. Both devices do need to be on the same Wi-Fi. There is no Bluetooth in this app, despite what the gesture suggests.

Your device name stays yours

Each install invents a permanent name of its own and announces that instead. Your real device name is never broadcast.

vivid-vole-4997

Nothing to learn

There's no menu bar flyout to hunt through and no window to arrange. If your hands are on the keyboard, they're already where the controls are.

A tested core, not a demo

The gesture engine is a pure Swift core with no platform dependencies at all, covered by 93 passing checks. Everything about hand shapes can be reasoned about without a camera in the room.

07 — Security

A camera app and a screen-sharing app in one. So here's exactly what it does.

Your camera feed never leaves your Mac.

Hand tracking runs entirely on-device through Apple's Vision framework. Frames are analysed in memory and discarded. No video is recorded, stored, or transmitted — not to another device, not to me. What travels is the outcome: "a hand with four fingers extended was seen."

It goes to one device, after two deliberate acts.

A screen is handed over by a gesture on the sending Mac, and caught by a gesture on the receiving device — which you approved once, beforehand. There is no way to take something from a device that hasn't offered it, and no way to push something onto a device that hasn't asked.

Traffic between Apple devices is encrypted.

macOS refuses to form an unencrypted session, so Mac-to-Mac and Mac-to-iPad connections are encrypted by construction, and go straight between your devices with no server in the path. The experimental Windows bridge is not encrypted — treat it as a lab tool, not a private channel.

No accounts, no telemetry, no analytics.

The app has no sign-in, sends no usage data, and makes no outbound internet connections. This website has no trackers and sets no cookies.

The permissions it asks for, and why

Camera
to read gestures. On-device only. Required.
Screen Rec
to mirror a window and to take screenshots. Optional: links work fine without it.
Local Network
to find nearby devices over Bonjour and talk to them directly.
Automation
the first time you hand off a link, to read the front tab's URL and close it. Granted per browser, by you, in a macOS prompt.

What I'm straightforward about

Link handoff needs Apple Events, which means the app runs outside the App Sandbox. That's a real trade-off, and there's no way to do this feature without it.

The Mac build is signed but not notarized, so macOS warns on first open. Notarization needs the paid Apple Developer Program — the same membership that would make an iPhone or iPad install possible at all. Until then, the honest position is: this is a personal project you're choosing to trust. The source is public so you don't have to take my word for any of the above.

iPad and iPhone

Two routes, both through Apple. Build it onto your own device with Xcode — with a free Apple account the app stops working after seven days and has to be rebuilt. Or wait for TestFlight, which needs the $99/yr Developer Program.

That same membership is what removes the Gatekeeper warning on the Mac build, so it buys both things at once.

experimental

A Python bridge that talks to a headless peer on the Mac, not to the Mac app.

Run python doctor.py first — it checks your Python version, every dependency, whether your camera actually produces a frame, and whether the right port is free, then tells you which failures matter.

FAQ

Does my camera stay on the whole time?

While the app is open, yes — that's how it sees your hand. Frames are analysed in memory and thrown away immediately. Nothing is recorded, saved, or sent anywhere. Quit the app and the camera light goes out.

Can someone hand me something without me knowing?

No. Two things have to happen: they gesture to offer it, and you gesture to catch it. A device also has to be approved by you the first time it ever tries. Nothing can be pushed onto you.

Does it need the internet?

No. Both devices need to be on the same Wi-Fi, but nothing leaves your network and there's no server anywhere in the path.

What exactly gets shared — my whole desktop?

No. One window: the one you were in when you closed your hand. There's deliberately no whole-desktop option.

Will it accidentally trigger while I'm typing?

It needs a deliberate, held gesture — a flicker won't do it. And while it's holding a page, gestures are ignored entirely so stray hands can't cancel it. If you're on a live video call it stops and asks first, and doing nothing means no.

What if nobody catches it?

The page comes back after 20 seconds. And if there's no device nearby at all, it refuses to take the page in the first place.

Can I get it on my iPhone or iPad?

Only by building it yourself with Xcode, and it expires after seven days on a free Apple account. This isn't a limitation of the app — Apple provides no way to install an iOS app from a website. TestFlight would fix it and needs a paid membership.

Does it work with Windows?

Halfway. A Windows PC will find a Mac and connect to it — that part is verified on a real machine. Handing links across, and the camera gestures, haven't been tested yet. It's labelled experimental for a reason.

Is it really free?

Yes, and it stays free. There's no pro tier, no waitlist, no email capture.
